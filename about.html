About

I work at the intersection of computational statistics, computer science, sociology, and international policy.

Current directionPhD preparation in computational statistics
Professional contextState Department / United Nations
Core toolsR · Python

I am interested in statistical methods that remain useful when the world is messy: incomplete information, high-stakes decisions, complex institutions, and imperfect measurement.

My academic interests center on applied computational statistics and the use of quantitative methods to understand human systems. I am particularly interested in problems related to humanitarian assistance, counterterrorism, deradicalization, and emerging technology governance.

In my professional work, I apply statistics and computation in U.S. Department of State and United Nations contexts to support real-world policy and decision-making, including Ambassador-level advising. The purpose of the Projects section is to document that applied work without confusing it with academic research.

The common thread is methodological: how to extract useful, defensible information from difficult data and communicate it clearly enough to change a decision.
