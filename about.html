About

The life around the work.

The rest of this site explains what I study and build. This page is about the experiences, places, and interests that shaped the person doing it.

I have spent much of my adult life moving between universities, military service, unfamiliar countries, mountains, and technical projects. Outside research, I am usually reading, hiking, building something unnecessary on a computer, or planning somewhere else to go. My family roots are in Norway and Italy; I have crossed nearly every U.S. state and national park, along with travel through Japan, Morocco, France, Algeria, and Spain.

01 / Military Service

U.S. Army

Before graduate school, I served as a paratrooper and squad leader in an airborne infantry unit. It remains one of the most formative parts of my background.

I was responsible for leading soldiers, maintaining accountability and readiness, and communicating clearly in environments where incomplete information, physical stress, and changing conditions were routine. Much of that experience took place in Alaska and included Arctic and extreme-cold-weather training and operations.

Military service changed the way I approach difficult problems. I learned to distinguish what is known from what is assumed, communicate decisions in plain language, and prefer solutions that remain useful outside ideal conditions. The CV contains the formal chronology; here, the more important point is that the Army shaped how I think about leadership, uncertainty, and responsibility.

02 / Outside the Model

Mountains & movement

Hiking and mountaineering are my main counterweight to screen-bound work: long approaches, exposed ridgelines, cold weather, unusual geology, and the perspective that comes from seeing a place at human scale.

Travel sits naturally beside the mountains for me. I have visited nearly every U.S. state and national park, as well as Japan, Morocco, France, Algeria, and Spain. The two most beautiful things I have seen are still the aurora borealis and cherry blossoms—one enormous and almost unreal, the other brief enough to make its beauty feel conditional.

04 / Off the Clock

The rest of it

Not every interest needs an interface. These are simply the things I tend to reach for when work is finished.

I listen mostly to punk, metal, and rock; watch a lot of horror; and gravitate toward anime and games with strong atmosphere—especially Eighty-Six, Chainsaw Man, and Dark Souls. I also have a longstanding interest in folklore, cryptids, demonology, and paranormal history, which I hope to turn into a casual podcast.

Most of my actual downtime is simpler: reading, getting outside, tinkering with something technical, and spending time at home with my longtime partner and cats.

The work matters. So does having a life large enough that the work is only part of it.
