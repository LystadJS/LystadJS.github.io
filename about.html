My priorities center around addressing high-stakes situations using statistical methods that remain useful when the world is messy: incomplete information, invisible covariates, complex interactions, and imperfect measurement.

My academic interests lie at the crossroads where applied computational statistics, quantitative methods, and social systems intersect. I am particularly interested in problems related to humanitarian crises, counterterrorism, deradicalization, and emerging technology.

In my professional work, I apply computational statistics in support of the U.S. Mission to the United Nations, bridging the disconnect between data and policy to aid diplomacy in high-stakes situations.

The common thread is methodological: how to extract useful, defensible information from difficult data and communicate it clearly enough to guide decision-making and save lives.
