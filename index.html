I use and develop statistical and computational methods for problems in which data are incomplete, structure is latent, systems change over time, and errors carry real consequences. My research centers on unsupervised learning, missing-data methods, longitudinal and network models, forecasting, and causal evaluation, with applications to human security, political violence, humanitarian crises, climate risk, and emerging technologies. Across these domains, I aim to recover defensible structure from messy data, quantify uncertainty, and build models that remain interpretable, reproducible, and useful for high-stakes decisions.

      02 / Research Direction

      Anticipatory statistical science

      Recognize danger.
      Change the trajectory.

      I develop statistical methods to detect emerging danger, forecast where and when systems may fail, and evaluate interventions that could prevent catastrophe or reduce harm once a crisis begins.

      Prediction ≠ prevention. Forecasting identifies risk. Prevention requires evidence about where action can still change the outcome.

      03 / Failure Modes

      What would make
      the warning wrong?

      The research standard: make uncertainty, failure, and the consequences of error part of the analysis.

      01 / Measurement

      The signal is not the phenomenon.

      Test whether missing data, reporting changes, or imperfect proxies create the apparent warning.

      02 / Model

      The pattern depends on the assumptions.

      Check sensitivity, misspecification, leakage, and performance outside the data used to build the model.

      03 / Shift

      The system no longer behaves the same way.

      Examine changing relationships, new conditions, and actors adapting to observation or intervention.

      04 / Action

      A correct warning can still lead to the wrong response.

      Evaluate feasibility, causal effects, unintended consequences, and who bears the cost of mistakes.

      Evaluate both sides of error. Missed catastrophes and false alarms both matter. So do the distribution of harm and the time available to respond.

      04 / Correspondence

      Methods, anticipation, and collaboration.

      I welcome conversations about latent structure, dynamic systems, and statistical methods for anticipating and responding to severe risks.
