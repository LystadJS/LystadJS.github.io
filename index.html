      02 / Research Direction

      Anticipatory statistical science

      Recognize danger.
      Change the trajectory.

      I am building a research program around a central question: how can we anticipate catastrophic events early enough to prevent them, and respond more effectively when they have already begun?

      My focus is the statistical work between a weak signal and a useful decision: recovering hidden structure, forecasting dangerous transitions, and evaluating when, where, and how intervention could reduce harm.

      A research direction.
      Not a claim of perfect foresight.
      1. I

        Detect

        What state are we in?

        Recover hidden structure and emerging warning signals from incomplete, noisy observations.

        Latent structure · measurement · unsupervised learning
      2. II

        Forecast

        Where is it heading?

        Estimate the probability, timing, location, and pathways of dangerous change.

        Longitudinal models · event histories · dynamic networks
      3. III

        Intervene

        Can the path change?

        Identify actionable windows and assess which interventions could alter the outcome.

        Causal inference · decision theory · intervention evaluation
      4. IV

        Adapt

        What do we know now?

        Update inference and response as new evidence arrives, including after a crisis begins.

        Sequential inference · updating · response evaluation

      03 / Open Questions

      The questions
      behind the program.

      A connected set of statistical problems, rather than a catalogue of application areas. Expand a question to see the methodological focus.

      01Can we detect danger before a crisis has a label?

      Study how latent-variable models and unsupervised learning can identify candidate warning signals, then test whether those signals anticipate meaningful outcomes.

      02When is apparent structural change real?

      Separate changes in the underlying system from sampling variation, measurement error, and movement introduced by the representation itself.

      03Which local failures can become wider cascades?

      Investigate how network structure, dependencies, and changing relationships shape potential pathways from localized disruption to broader harm.

      04How much warning is enough to act?

      Connect forecast horizons to feasible response times, balancing missed events, false alarms, uncertainty, and the cost of intervention.

      05Which intervention changes the outcome?

      Use causal designs and explicit assumptions to compare possible actions, distinguish intervention effects from selection, and identify where evidence is insufficient.

      06How should decisions change once a crisis begins?

      Develop ways to update estimates and evaluate responses as conditions shift and incomplete or delayed evidence becomes available.

      04 / Analysis Cycle

      Anticipation is a loop.
      Not a one-off prediction.

      Select a stage to inspect the task and the question that keeps the inference accountable.

      ↶ New evidence returns to observation. Decisions and outcomes inform the next cycle.

      01 / Observe

      Establish what is actually observed.

      Assemble indicators, document their provenance, and examine missingness, delay, and changes in measurement.

      Inferential check

      Are we measuring a change in the system—or a change in how it is recorded?

      02 / Infer state

      Recover structure without treating it as ground truth.

      Estimate latent states, groups, and relationships, carrying uncertainty from the observations into the representation.

      Inferential check

      Does the inferred structure survive alternative measurements, representations, and assumptions?

      03 / Forecast

      Describe possible futures, with uncertainty.

      Estimate timing, location, and pathways of risk over a defined horizon; evaluate calibration and warning time on held-out periods.

      Inferential check

      Would this warning have been available early enough to matter, using only evidence known at that time?

      04 / Compare actions

      Ask what an action could actually change.

      Compare feasible interventions using causal evidence, explicit assumptions, and the costs of action and inaction.

      Inferential check

      What identifies the intervention effect, and could the action shift harm elsewhere?

      05 / Update

      Revise the model as the situation changes.

      Incorporate new observations, check for drift, and evaluate whether the response is changing outcomes as intended.

      Inferential check

      Has the system—or our intervention—changed the relationships on which the forecast depended?

      Prediction ≠ prevention.

      A forecast asks what is likely to happen. Causal evaluation asks what would change under an intervention.

      My aim is to connect these questions without treating predictive accuracy as proof of preventive effectiveness.

      05 / Failure Modes

      What would make
      the warning wrong?

      The research standard: make uncertainty, failure, and the consequences of error part of the analysis.

      01 / Measurement

      The signal is not the phenomenon.

      Test whether missing data, reporting changes, or imperfect proxies create the apparent warning.

      02 / Model

      The pattern depends on the assumptions.

      Check sensitivity, misspecification, leakage, and performance outside the data used to build the model.

      03 / Shift

      The system no longer behaves the same way.

      Examine changing relationships, new conditions, and actors adapting to observation or intervention.

      04 / Action

      A correct warning can still lead to the wrong response.

      Evaluate feasibility, causal effects, unintended consequences, and who bears the cost of mistakes.

      Evaluate both sides of error. Missed catastrophes and false alarms both matter. So do the distribution of harm and the time available to respond.

      06 / Current Methods Problem

      When a map moves,
      did the system change?

      Methodological building block · In development

      My current methods work examines how to compare repeated low-dimensional maps, distinguish coordinate changes from structural change, and carry uncertainty into that comparison.

      For anticipatory analysis, this is a prior question: before interpreting movement as a warning, establish whether it reflects the system or the way the map was constructed.
