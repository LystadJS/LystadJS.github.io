A portfolio of scholarly work applying statistical and computational methods to substantive questions in social science, counterterrorism research, and human security with particular attention to inference under difficult real-world data conditions.

Published May 2026

The Caliphate's Target Map: Ethnosectarian Composition and Insurgent Violence in Iraq

Examined whether Islamic State attack patterns in Iraq aligned with its sectarian ideology by linking insurgent violence to district-level ethnosectarian composition, population-weighted geography, and target-type modeling. Identified concentrated attack activity in Sunni and religiously fractionalized areas, alongside elevated civilian targeting in non-Sunni districts.

The Effects of Climate Change on the Development of Terrorist Networks in the Middle East and North Africa

Analyzed how projected changes in temperature, precipitation, evaporation, and heat stress could intensify resource scarcity, weaken governance, and create conditions exploitable by terrorist organizations in the Middle East. Used NASA Global Climate Model scenario comparisons to connect environmental change with recruitment dynamics and regional instability.

02 / Agenda

Research Agenda

A developing academic agenda focused on how statistical and computational methods can be implemented into social science research when data is incomplete, uncertainty is consequential, and the systems being studied are complex, human, and policy-relevant.

Inference

What survives bad data?

How should inference change when data are missing, biased, heterogeneous, or produced for operational rather than research purposes?

Governance

How does openness change proliferation?

What changes when strategically relevant technical capability diffuses through open-source models, code, and tooling?

Human systems

How can risk be measured responsibly?

What can statistical models contribute when the objects of analysis are people, institutions, and high-consequence environments?

Translation

When does evidence change a decision?

How should uncertainty be communicated when analysis must move from model output to policy or practice?
